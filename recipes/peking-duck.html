Authentic Peking Duck

Description

Peking Duck is a famous dish from Beijing characterized by its thin, crisp, shatteringly flavorful skin. Authentic versions involve separating the skin from the meat with air, glazing the skin with sweet syrup, drying the bird completely, and roasting it to a deep mahogany color. It is traditionally carved and served wrapped in thin mandarin pancakes with sweet bean sauce and fresh scallions.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole duck (approx. 5-6 lbs)
  • 4 tablespoons honey or maltose syrup
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 piece of fresh ginger, sliced
  • 2 whole star anise
  • 1 pack of Chinese mandarin pancakes
  • 1 bunch of scallions (green onions), sliced into thin strips
  • 1 cucumber, seeded and julienned
  • 1/2 cup Hoisin sauce or Sweet Bean Sauce (Tianmian醬)

Steps

  1. Clean the duck thoroughly, pat dry inside and out, and tie a string around its neck.
  2. Bring a large pot of water to a boil with the ginger and star anise, then carefully ladle the boiling water all over the duck's skin until it tightens.
  3. Mix the honey (or maltose) with the soy sauce and a splash of warm water to create a glaze. Brush this glaze evenly over the entire surface of the duck skin.
  4. Hang the duck in a cool, breezy place (or place it on a wire rack in the refrigerator uncovered) for 24 hours to completely dry out the skin.
  5. Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C) and roast the duck on a rack inside a roasting pan for 1 hour to 1.5 hours, turning once, until the skin is deep golden-brown and crispy.
  6. Let the duck rest for 10 minutes, then carefully carve slices containing both crisp skin and juicy meat.
  7. Assemble by smearing Hoisin sauce on a warm mandarin pancake, adding duck slices, scallions, and cucumber, then rolling it up to eat.
